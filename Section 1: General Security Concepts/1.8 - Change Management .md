# 🔄 Change Management / Change Control (Simple Notes)

In a home setup, a change usually affects **one computer**.  
In a company, **one change can affect hundreds or thousands of systems**.

That’s why organizations use **change management**.

---

## **Why Change Control Matters**
- Systems need updates to stay **secure**
- Random changes can:
  - Break apps
  - Cause outages
  - Create confusion
- A formal process keeps systems **stable and available**

---

## **What Is Change Management?**
A **structured process** for making changes to:
- Applications
- Operating systems
- Network devices (routers, firewalls)
- Software updates and patches

---

## **Goals of Change Control**
- Keep systems running (uptime)
- Reduce mistakes
- Make sure everyone is informed
- Know how to undo a change if needed

---

## **Typical Change Control Steps**

1. **Submit a change request**
   - Why the change is needed
   - What systems are affected
   - Scope (small or large)

2. **Schedule the change**
   - Date and time
   - Expected impact

3. **Risk analysis**
   - What could break?
   - What happens if we don’t make the change?

4. **Approval**
   - Change control board decides approve / deny

5. **Implement the change**

6. **Test and verify**
   - Users confirm systems work

---

## **Roles Involved**

### **Owner**
- Owns the application or data
- Requests the change
- Tests after the change

### **IT Team**
- Performs the actual change

### **Stakeholders**
- Anyone affected by the change
- May include:
  - Other departments
  - Finance
  - Management
  - Executives

> Small changes can affect many people.

---

## **Risk Considerations**
- Change may:
  - Fix nothing
  - Break something else
  - Cause downtime
- Not changing can be risky too:
  - Security vulnerabilities
  - System failures

---

## **Testing with a Sandbox**
- Test changes in a **safe environment**
- Sandbox = copy of production
- No risk to real users
- Good place to test:
  - Updates
  - Patches
  - Rollback plans

---

## **Backout / Rollback Plan**
- Plan to **undo the change**
- Examples:
  - Uninstall patch
  - Restore config
  - Roll back software
- Always have **backups** before changes

---

## **Timing the Change**
- Avoid busy work hours
- Often done:
  - Nights
  - Weekends
  - Holidays
- Some businesses freeze changes during busy seasons

---

## **Key Takeaway**
Change management:
- Protects systems
- Protects users
- Protects the business

No changes should happen **without approval and planning**.
